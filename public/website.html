Frequently Asked Questions

Answers to common queries regarding preparation courses, recorded lessons, assignment reviews, and extended material access.

What courses are available and what are their durations?
We offer three preparation options: the 1-Month Long-Term Batch, the 15-Day Short-Term Course, and the 1-Week Crash Course. Each course covers Listening, Reading, Writing, and Speaking according to your exam timeline.
How do live recorded lessons and study resources work?
All live class sessions are recorded in high definition and uploaded to the Student Portal. You can access recordings, presentation decks, practice worksheets, and exercise keys at any time from your computer or mobile browser.
How are assignments submitted and evaluated?
Students submit written essays or recorded speaking responses directly through the Student Portal. The tutor evaluates each submission and provides line-by-line feedback across Task Achievement, Coherence & Cohesion, Lexical Resource, and Grammatical Range.
What is the 3-month extended material access buffer?
Upon completing any course, students automatically receive three additional calendar months of access to all recorded lessons, notes, and study resources. This buffer ensures you can continue revising up until your scheduled test date. Assignment evaluation support during this buffer period is managed separately.
How can I enquire about course fees and enrollment?
Course fees are shared upon request. Simply click "Contact Us" to submit an enquiry specifying your preferred preparation timeline and target test date.

Writing Task 2

Top 10 Grammar Mistakes in IELTS Writing Task 2

Article Guidance • 6 min read

Grammatical Range and Accuracy is one of four equal criteria evaluated in IELTS Writing. Many test candidates assume using sophisticated vocabulary will offset weak sentence syntax, but examiners quickly identify systemic grammatical errors.

1. Missing Definite and Indefinite Articles

Writing "Government should invest in technology" instead of "The government should invest in technology" remains one of the most frequent errors in candidate essays.

2. Faulty Subject-Verb Agreement

When complex relative clauses separate a subject from its verb, candidates frequently confuse plural noun modifiers with singular main subjects.

3. Overusing Passive Voice Unnaturally

While academic tone requires passive constructions where appropriate, forcing every sentence into passive voice creates clumsy phrasing.

Speaking Part 2

How to Master IELTS Speaking Part 2 Cue Cards in 60 Seconds

Article Guidance • 5 min read

In Part 2 of the IELTS Speaking test, you receive a task card and 1 minute to prepare a 2-minute speech. Many candidates spend this time trying to write full sentences, running out of time before outlining main ideas.

The Past, Present, Future Framework

Organise your 1-minute brain map into three chronological zones:

  • Past: How you first encountered or experienced the topic.
  • Present: Current details, feelings, or descriptions.
  • Future: Why this topic will remain significant to you in years to come.

Following this timeline ensures sufficient content to comfortably speak for 120 seconds without long pauses.
